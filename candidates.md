# Candidates — Everyday Things, Two Lines, Longer Run

Competition: `u-suga-everyday-things-two-lines-longer-run-f8734479`
Gate: entry text must be UTF-8, 1–400 characters, exactly two lines.
Judge brief (in order): (1) really explains one everyday thing to a 10-year-old,
one vivid sentence + a second sentence on the mapping; (2) analogy concrete and
surprising — things a 10-year-old can see, hold or stand in, joined in a way not
met before; (3) line 1 is one vivid sentence, whole picture, no list, no second
metaphor; (4) line 2 honest and specific about what the analogy maps and what it
leaves out — no praising; (5) the pair works together, in language a 10-year-old
follows without being talked down to. A vivid picture that leaves the wrong idea
is worse than a plain one that holds.

Playbook applied (from the winners study): one concrete bodily scene, second
person, present tense, sensory detail; avoid the stock analogies every other
entry will use (toaster = dragon, rain = sky crying, traffic = river).

## The candidates

### C1 — Queue as a train you stand in
Line 1: A queue is a train you stand in: you are one carriage in a line of people, and the whole train shuffles toward the front one small step at a time.
Line 2: The train explains the single file and the slow shuffle, but it leaves out that you are not hooked to anyone — you can step out whenever you like, and the line just closes behind you.

### C2 — Shadow as a flat copy
Line 1: Your shadow is a flat, silent copy of you that the sun stamps onto the ground: it copies every move you make, but it can never lift off the floor.
Line 2: The copy explains why it matches your shape and follows your moves, but it leaves out that a shadow is not a thing you could touch — it is only the patch of ground your body kept the sunlight from reaching.

### C3 — Elevator as a climbing room
Line 1: An elevator is a room that climbs: you step in, the doors close, and the whole little room slides up inside the building while you stand still and do nothing.
Line 2: The climbing room explains the ride and the waiting, but it leaves out the hidden cables and motor that haul the room up — the room never climbs by itself.

### C4 — Fridge as a box of winter
Line 1: A fridge is a box of winter that never ends: inside it stays cold all year, so the germs on your food move too slowly to spoil it fast.
Line 2: Winter explains the cold and the slow spoiling, but it leaves out that winter is weather that arrives on its own — the fridge's cold is made by a humming motor, and it stops the day the plug comes out.

### C5 — Traffic jam as a stretched parking lot
Line 1: A traffic jam is a parking lot stretched along a road: hundreds of cars all dressed for a journey, parked bumper to bumper and going nowhere.
Line 2: The parking lot explains the crowding and the stillness, but it leaves out why nobody can simply leave — each car is trapped only because the car in front of it is trapped too.

### C6 — Echo as catch with a cliff
Line 1: An echo is your shout playing catch with a cliff: you throw your voice at the rock, and it comes bouncing straight back to you, fainter every time.
Line 2: Catch explains the voice coming back and wearing out, but it leaves out that the cliff has no hands and throws nothing — your sound simply bounces off the hard rock.

### C7 — Hiccups as a jump in the chest
Line 1: A hiccup is a jump inside your chest: a sheet of muscle under your ribs twitches without asking you, and each twitch snaps a gulp of air out with a squeak.
Line 2: The jump explains the sudden jolt and the squeak, but it leaves out that nothing is leaping — the muscle is only tightening for a split second, over and over, until it calms down on its own.

### C8 — Zipper as hugging teeth
Line 1: A zipper is two rows of tiny teeth that hug each other shut when the slider walks them together, and let go one pair at a time when it walks back.
Line 2: The teeth explain the tight grip and the neat opening, but they leave out that real teeth bite once and hold — zipper teeth can hug and let go a thousand times and never get tired.

### C9 — Microwave as rubbing hands
Line 1: A microwave warms food the way rubbing your hands together warms them: it shakes the water hiding inside the food so fast that the shaking itself turns into heat.
Line 2: The hand-rubbing maps heat made by movement instead of fire, but it leaves out that no hands ever touch the food — invisible waves do the shaking, and they only shake the water, not the plate.

### C10 — Toaster as a sun-bed for bread
Line 1: A toaster is a sun-bed for a slice of bread: two glowing orange walls hug it until its pale skin turns golden and crisp.
Line 2: The sun-bed explains the glow and the browning on both sides, but it leaves out that nothing is tanning — the heat is drying the bread out, which is why toast left too long comes out burnt, not browner.

## The duel

Judged head-to-head on the brief's criteria, in the brief's order.

- **C10 (toaster sun-bed)** falls first: the picture risks the wrong idea —
  bread does not tan, and "sun-bed" is a stretch a 10-year-old may not know.
- **C9 (microwave)** is accurate but the analogy is the textbook one; the judge
  has met it before, so it loses on criterion 2 (surprise).
- **C8 (zipper)** is charming, but line 2's "never get tired" is praise dressed
  as honesty, and the mapping (grip) is thinner than the leaders'.
- **C4 (fridge)** — "winter in a box" is a familiar join; loses on surprise.
- **C6 (echo)** — catch with a cliff is concrete and fun, but the cliff "playing
  catch" slightly misleads before line 2 repairs it; line 2 is also shorter on
  what the analogy *maps* (only the return, not the fading's cause).
- **C7 (hiccups)** — bodily and vivid, but "a jump" is the weakest central image
  of the survivors; the thing explained is also the least "everyday object".
- **C5 (traffic jam)** — strong, honest line 2 (the trap is recursive), but a
  parking lot is static: line 1 explains the stillness better than the *jam*,
  and the image is one a 10-year-old mostly sees from inside a car, not stands in.
- **C3 (elevator)** — very concrete, you literally stand in it; loses the
  semifinal to C1 because the "room that climbs" join is one most kids have
  already half-formed, and its line 2 lists machinery rather than naming a
  mapping limit that changes how you picture the ride.
- **Final: C2 (shadow) vs C1 (queue).** C2's line 2 is the most philosophically
  honest of the ten ("not a thing at all"), but its line 1 image — a copy of
  you on the ground — is the more familiar picture, and "stamps" quietly
  credits the sun with making something rather than blocking light. C1 wins on
  criterion 2: a train *you stand in*, where you are one carriage, is a join a
  10-year-old has not met before, it is bodily (standing, shuffling), and its
  line 2 names both the mapping (single file, slow shuffle) and a specific,
  checkable limit (you are not hooked; you can step out and the line closes
  behind you). C1 also wins criterion 5: both lines speak to "you" throughout.

## Winner

**C1 — Queue as a train you stand in** (see main.py for the final text).
